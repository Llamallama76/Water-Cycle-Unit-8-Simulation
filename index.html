SC.5.E.7.1 — Parts of the Water Cycle & States of Matter

Water moves continuously through Evaporation (liquid to gas powered by the Sun), Condensation (cooling gas to liquid cloud droplets or ice crystals), Precipitation, and Surface Runoff. Water changes back and forth among gas, liquid, and solid.

SC.5.E.7.2 — Role of the Ocean

Holding about 97% of Earth's water, the ocean is connected to all freshwater reservoirs (lakes, rivers, mountain ice) through evaporation and precipitation. When ocean water evaporates, salt stays behind so rainwater is fresh!

SC.5.E.7.3 & SC.5.E.7.4 — Weather Factors & 4 Forms of Precipitation

Air temperature, humidity, and wind determine local weather and distinguish the four tested forms of precipitation: Rain (liquid drops above freezing), Snow (solid ice crystals falling through freezing air), Sleet (raindrops freezing into ice pellets in a cold air layer near the ground), and Hail (layered ice balls formed by strong thunderstorm updrafts).

Florida Statewide Science Assessment Scope & Limits
